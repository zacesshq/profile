Reading the night sky

On a clear night away from street lights, a few thousand stars are visible. Most of the bright ones belong to a handful of patterns that are easy to learn. Once those are known the rest of the sky falls into place around them.

Start with the Plough, seven stars that sit high in the north for most of the year. The two at the end of its bowl point almost straight at the Pole Star, which barely moves through the night. Read more in our guide to the northern sky.

From there, follow the curve of the handle away from the bowl to find Arcturus, a bright orange star that is easy to spot in spring. Keep going in the same direction to reach Spica, low in the south.
